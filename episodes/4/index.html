Renting an Apartment in English

How do you ask about renting an apartment in English? The English you need to find a place, visit it and talk to a landlord: rent, bills, viewings, the deposit and the lease.

About this episode

Episode 4 · 3 min · Level A2–B1 · Hosts: Lina and Adam

The English you need to find a place, visit it and talk to a landlord: rent, bills, viewings, the deposit and the lease.

Phrases in this episode

  • “Is the apartment still available?”
  • “How much is the rent per month?”
  • “Are bills included?”
  • “Can I come for a viewing this week?”
  • “How long is the lease?”
  • “Could you explain this part, please?”

Transcript

Lina: Hi everyone, and welcome to Cadence Talk! I'm Lina.

Adam: And I'm Adam. Today we're talking about something big: renting your first apartment.

Lina: Oh, I remember my first apartment. It was tiny, but I loved it.

Adam: Tiny is fine! Today we'll learn the English you need to find a place and talk to a landlord.

Lina: Let's start with the first step. You see an apartment online and you want to call.

Adam: Right. A great first phrase is: "Is the apartment still available?"

Lina: "Is the apartment still available?" Simple and polite.

Adam: Then you ask about money. You can say: "How much is the rent per month?"

Lina: And don't forget to ask: "Are bills included?"

Adam: Good one. Bills are things like water, electricity and internet.

Lina: Sometimes the rent looks cheap, but the bills are not included.

Adam: Exactly. So always ask. Then you can visit the place. We call this a viewing.

Lina: "Can I come for a viewing this week?"

Adam: Perfect. At the viewing, look carefully. Check the windows, the water and the heating.

Lina: And ask questions. For example: "Is it furnished?"

Adam: Furnished means it has furniture, like a bed, a sofa and a table.

Lina: If it's empty, we say it's unfurnished.

Adam: Now, the landlord might talk about a deposit.

Lina: A deposit is money you pay at the start. You get it back when you leave, if everything is okay.

Adam: So a smart question is: "How much is the deposit, and when do I get it back?"

Lina: I love that question. It shows you are careful.

Adam: Next is the contract. In English we often call it the lease.

Lina: "How long is the lease?" Six months? One year?

Adam: And please read it before you sign. Every line.

Lina: Yes! If you don't understand something, say: "Could you explain this part, please?"

Adam: That's a very useful phrase everywhere, not only for renting.

Lina: Let's try a short role play. Adam, you're the landlord.

Adam: Okay! Hello, you called about the apartment?

Lina: Yes, hi! Is the apartment still available?

Adam: It is. The rent is eight hundred a month.

Lina: Are bills included?

Adam: Water is included, but electricity and internet are not.

Lina: I see. Is it furnished?

Adam: Yes, it has a bed, a sofa and a small desk.

Lina: Great. Can I come for a viewing this week?

Adam: Sure. How about Thursday at five?

Lina: Thursday at five is perfect. Thank you!

Adam: Nice job! Clear, polite and friendly.

Lina: Okay, quick review time. Here are today's phrases.

Adam: One: "Is the apartment still available?"

Lina: Two: "How much is the rent per month?"

Adam: Three: "Are bills included?"

Lina: Four: "Can I come for a viewing this week?"

Adam: Five: "How long is the lease?"

Lina: And six: "Could you explain this part, please?"

Adam: Say them out loud with us. Practice makes them easy.

Lina: Now a question for you. What is the most important thing for you in a home? Tell us in the comments!

Adam: Thanks for listening, everyone.

Lina: See you tomorrow on Cadence Talk!
